# CATIS Framework

I kept running into the same problem from different angles over the years: the people securing an application, the people running the network under it, and whoever owns the connected devices at the edge almost never plan together. That gap between them is usually where things go wrong.

CATIS is what I've been building to deal with that. It's a six-function approach — Govern, Map, Validate, Secure, Detect & Respond, Sustain — for treating that handoff as one thing to manage, instead of three separate jobs done by three separate teams. It isn't meant to replace the standards you're probably already using, like NIST CSF, IEC 62351, or ISO 27001. It sits on top of them and tries to close a specific gap those standards leave open.

## What's here

`CATIS_Framework_v2.5.pdf` has the full write-up: the six functions, a diagram of how it's structured, a look at where NISTIR 7628 and IEC 62351 fall short and how CATIS answers those specific gaps, a table mapping CATIS to standards you'd recognize, a few sector examples (health, power, manufacturing, financial services), and a glossary at the end for anyone who doesn't want to look up every acronym.
